While critics raise valid concerns about image curation, arguing that social media influencers are uniquely promoting "unrealistic" beauty standards overlooks several crucial points.

First, beauty standards are not a social media invention. Decades before influencers existed, magazines, television, and film presented equally narrow—and often digitally enhanced—ideals. The difference is that social media actually offers MORE diversity than traditional media. Followers can choose to follow body-positive influencers, plus-size advocates, age-diverse creators, and those promoting authentic, unfiltered content. The algorithm responds to what users engage with, giving individuals agency over their feed.

Second, many influencers are actively pushing back against unrealistic standards. The rise of authenticity culture—where creators share unfiltered photos, discuss mental health, and reject perfection—demonstrates that the influencer space is not monolithic. Brands increasingly seek "real" people over airbrushed models, indicating a market shift toward relatability.

Third, the argument assumes followers passively absorb content without critical thinking. This underestimates media literacy. Young people are not helpless victims; they can recognize editing, question standards, and develop resilience. Blaming influencers alone ignores the role of parenting, education, and personal choice.

Finally, "unattainable" is subjective. What seems unrealistic to one person may be achievable for another through legitimate lifestyle choices. Fitness influencers, for instance, may inspire genuine health improvements.

In summary, while some influencers curate unrealistic images, the platform itself offers choice, diversity, and counter-narratives. Blaming influencers for broader societal beauty standards misplaces responsibility and ignores the complex, user-driven nature of social media.
