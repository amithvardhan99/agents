Social media influencers are indeed promoting unrealistic beauty standards. Their feeds are saturated with heavily filtered, Photoshop‑perfect images that showcase an narrowly defined ideal—flawless skin, ultra‑thin or hyper‑toned bodies, exaggerated facial proportions—that is virtually impossible for the average person to attain without extensive digital editing or cosmetic procedures. This curated aesthetic is not only unattainable, it is actively amplified by the influencer business model, which rewards higher engagement with ever‑more perfect visuals and sells products that promise to help followers achieve those looks. The result is millions of followers, especially young people, internalising these impossible ideals, leading to widespread body dissatisfaction and a distorted perception of what is realistic. By constantly presenting and monetising these exaggerated standards, influencers directly contribute to the spread of unrealistic beauty expectations.
